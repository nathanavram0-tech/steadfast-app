## Summary
In two experiments, Sophie Leroy found that when people switch tasks, part of their attention stays stuck on the previous one ("attention residue"), and performance on the next task drops. Leaving the first task unfinished made the residue worse; finishing it under some time pressure helped people let go and do better on the next task.

## Practical application
- Work in single-task blocks (for example 60-90 minutes of editing) with notifications off and the phone in another room.
- Finish a clear chunk before switching, or write a one-line "next step" note so the brain can let go.
- Give tasks a deadline inside the block; mild time pressure helped people disengage.
- Batch email and messages into set times instead of checking between edits.

## Limitations
Lab experiments with short tasks; effect sizes in real long workdays are not established. The block lengths above are practical suggestions, not tested values.
