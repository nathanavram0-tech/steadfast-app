## Summary
Specific, difficult goals lead to better performance than easy or vague "do your best" goals. The effect is strongest when the person is committed, gets feedback on progress, and has the ability and strategy for the task.

## Practical application
- Make goals specific and measurable: "+15 lb lean by late February", "$10K/month from videography".
- Add feedback: weekly weigh-in average, monthly revenue check.
- For complex goals (building a business), set learning goals first ("test 3 offers this month") before outcome goals.

## Limitations
Very difficult goals without the skills to reach them can lead to shortcuts or burnout.
