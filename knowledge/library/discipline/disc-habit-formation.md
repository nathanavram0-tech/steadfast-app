## Summary
Habits do not form in 21 days. In a 12-week study of 96 people who picked a daily eating, drinking or exercise behavior, it took a median of 66 days for the behavior to become close to automatic, with a range of 18 to 254 days. Missing a single day did not meaningfully set people back.

## Key findings
- Automaticity rises quickly at first, then levels off.
- Simpler behaviors (drinking water) became automatic faster than harder ones (exercise).
- Same cue, same time, same place each day helped.

## Practical application
- Expect about 2-3 months of conscious effort before a new routine feels automatic. Plan for it.
- Anchor the habit to a fixed cue: "after I brush my teeth in the morning, I read one chapter".
- Never miss twice: one missed day does not break a habit, two starts a new pattern.
- Start smaller than you think, then grow it once it is automatic.

## Limitations
Small, self-reported study; many participants dropped data. The median hides huge individual variation.
