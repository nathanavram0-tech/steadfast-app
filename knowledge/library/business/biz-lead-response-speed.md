## Summary
An audit of how companies answered web inquiries, published in Harvard Business Review in 2011, concluded that most companies do not respond nearly fast enough, and that the chance of turning an inquiry into a real conversation falls quickly as hours pass. For a small studio, replying first and personally is one of the cheapest edges available.

## Practical application
- Reply to every wedding or business inquiry within the hour during working hours, and the same day at the latest.
- Use a saved first reply that is still personal: thank them, use their names and date, ask one question, and suggest a call time.
- Push for a short call: it's where trust and booking happen.
- Follow up 2-3 times over a week if they go quiet; most people are busy, not uninterested.
- Track inquiries, reply time and bookings so you know your conversion rate.

## Limitations
The exact figures from the HBR article could not be verified from the full text, so none are quoted here. The research looked at B2B web leads from 2011, not wedding couples; treat the "reply fast" principle as well-supported practice, not a measured booking rate.
