## Summary
The Knot's Real Weddings Study (10,474 U.S. couples married in 2025) put the average wedding videographer spend at $2,300. Spending ran $1,000 at the lower quartile, $1,900 at the median and $3,300 at the upper quartile. Regional averages ranged from about $2,100 (South, Southwest) to $3,000 (Mid-Atlantic). Price rises with hours of coverage, a second shooter, deliverables (social clips, cinematic feature, raw footage) and editing time.

## Practical application
- Build 3 packages around the market: an entry package near the median, a core package near the upper quartile, and a premium package above it for couples who want more coverage and a cinematic film.
- Price on deliverables and hours, not on a day rate alone; list exactly what each tier includes.
- Most buyers anchor on the middle option, so make the middle tier the one you most want to sell (see [[comm-first-offer-anchoring]]).
- Raise prices as the portfolio and demand grow; a full calendar months ahead is a signal you are underpriced.
- Count all your time (calls, travel, editing, revisions) and self-employment tax when checking if a price is profitable (see [[wealth-self-employment-tax]]).

## Limitations
These are what couples reported spending, not what profitable videographers should charge. Survey averages hide wide local variation and differences in quality. Package and anchoring advice is practical business reasoning, not tested in this survey.
