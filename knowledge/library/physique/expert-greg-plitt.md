## Summary
Greg Plitt (1977-2015) was a fitness model, trainer and motivational speaker from Maryland. He graduated from West Point (class of 2000), was Airborne and Ranger qualified and served five years as an Army Ranger. He then became one of the most-photographed fitness cover models (Muscle & Fitness, Men's Health, Men's Fitness and others), starred in Bravo's Work Out, and built a large following through intense motivational videos. He died on January 17, 2015, at 37, when he was struck by a train in Burbank, California, while filming a video.

## Expertise and achievements
- Military leadership and physical training background (West Point, Rangers).
- Created the MFT28 ("Military Fitness Trainer") 28-day program published on Bodybuilding.com, plus stair challenges like "1044 Steps to Paradise".
- MET-Rx athlete team member; their 2012 Athlete of the Year.
- His physique was used as a body reference for Dr. Manhattan in Watchmen (2009).

## Philosophy and teachings
- Training is a vehicle for character: every rep should mean something beyond looks.
- Do the hard things most people avoid; the difficult tasks are the least done and the most rewarding.
- Stay the course and keep focus; success compounds from consistent effort.
- Live so you can look back without regret, and ask how you want to be remembered.
- Take calculated risks rather than being ruled by fear.
- Build a personal brand by being genuinely yourself and using your personality to lift others.

## Proven strategies (what holds up)
- High-effort sessions, consistency and accountability: these line up with the research on proximity to failure and habit formation.
- Military-style structure (set times, set routines) is a sound way to remove daily decision-making.

## Failures and mistakes
- His death came from filming near an active train line. Reported accounts say he may have tried to outrun the train; this is reported, not confirmed. The lesson that applies: intensity is not the same as taking unnecessary physical risks.

## Evidence check
- His methods were coaching experience, not tested programs. Many of his workouts were very high volume and high intensity; that works for some, but research shows growth comes from enough hard sets, progression and recovery, not maximal punishment.
- No published data show MFT28 outcomes. Treat it as one structured program among many.

## Where his advice may be outdated
- Some period fitness media favored extreme cuts for shoots. For a lean bulk, slow gain and sleep matter more than photo-shoot leanness.

## Practical application
- Use his mindset as fuel for consistency and standards (show up, finish the set, do the hard thing first), and use evidence-based programming for the actual sets, reps and food.
- Pair motivation with systems: a scheduled session, a logged workout and a measured weekly result.
