## Summary
Sets need to be hard to grow muscle, but going all the way to failure on every set is not necessary. Growth tends to rise as sets get closer to failure, while strength gains are much less sensitive to it.

## Key findings
- Refalo 2023: training to failure and stopping short produced similar hypertrophy overall; the authors suggest stopping a few reps short is enough if sets are genuinely hard.
- Robinson 2024: hypertrophy increased as estimated reps in reserve went down (closer to failure); strength did not show the same relationship.

## Practical application
- Most working sets: 0-3 reps in reserve (you could do 0-3 more clean reps).
- Take the last set of isolation and machine exercises to failure; keep heavy barbell squats, deadlifts and bench a rep or two short for safety and recovery.
- Check yourself: if you often "had 5 more reps", the set was too easy to count as a hard set.

## Limitations
People are bad at guessing reps in reserve, especially on high-rep sets. Most data are short-term and in less-trained lifters.
