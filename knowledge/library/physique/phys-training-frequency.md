## Summary
When weekly sets are the same, training a muscle once, twice or three times a week grows it about equally. Frequency matters mainly as a tool to fit in enough quality volume: splitting 16 sets into two or three sessions keeps each set fresher than doing them all in one day.

## Key findings
- 2016 meta-analysis: training a muscle at least twice a week produced more growth than once, but many of those studies also differed in volume.
- 2019 meta-analysis, volume-equated: no meaningful difference in hypertrophy across frequencies.

## Practical application
- Hit each muscle about twice a week as a default. Split very high weekly volume across more sessions.
- Pick the split you will actually follow. Push/pull/legs twice a week, upper/lower four days, or full body three days all work.
- For a lagging muscle, adding a third short session with a few hard sets is an easy way to add volume without long workouts.

## Limitations
Studies are mostly 6-12 weeks and in young adults. Very high frequencies (5-6 times a week per muscle) are less studied.
