## Summary
A 2025 meta-analysis of 9 studies found no clear difference in muscle growth between one-side (unilateral) and two-side (bilateral) training, but strength followed specificity: bilateral training improved bilateral lifts more, and unilateral training improved single-limb strength more. The authors called the evidence limited, with moderate risk of bias.

## Practical application
- Small left-right differences are normal. Bring a lagging side up by giving it equal, honest work, not by doing extra sets only on that side forever.
- Use single-arm or single-leg versions (dumbbell press, one-arm cable row, split squats) for some of the work so the stronger side can't take over.
- Start each unilateral set with the weaker side and match its reps with the stronger side.
- Keep the big bilateral lifts if you want to get stronger at them, because strength is specific.
- Track it: arm and thigh tape measurements left vs right and photo scores (arm_left / arm_right) every few weeks.
- A large or sudden difference with pain, weakness or numbness is a reason to see a clinician, not to train harder.

## Limitations
Only 9 studies were included and the hypertrophy estimate had very wide uncertainty. No trial directly tested the best way to correct an existing asymmetry; the advice above is practical reasoning from the specificity finding.
