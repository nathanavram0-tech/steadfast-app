## Summary
More hard sets per muscle per week generally means more growth, up to a point. Schoenfeld's 2017 meta-analysis found a graded dose-response: about 10 or more weekly sets per muscle produced more growth than fewer. Newer analyses suggest the benefit keeps rising with volume but with diminishing returns, so each extra set buys less.

## Key findings
- Schoenfeld 2017: each added weekly set was associated with a small extra increase in muscle size; 10+ sets a week beat fewer than 5.
- Baz-Valle 2022: roughly 12-20 weekly sets per muscle looked like a sensible range for trained people; going higher did not reliably add more.
- Pelland 2024 (preprint, not yet peer reviewed when checked): growth kept increasing with volume with clear diminishing returns, while strength gains plateaued sooner.

## Practical application
- Count only hard sets (within about 0-3 reps of failure) for the muscle that is actually the main mover.
- Start a lagging muscle at about 10-12 hard sets a week and add 2-4 sets when progress stalls and recovery is good, rather than jumping to very high volume.
- If performance drops for 2+ weeks, joints ache, or sleep is poor, cut volume before adding more.
- For a bulk with limited time, put extra sets on the 1-3 muscles you most want to bring up, and keep the rest at maintenance-to-moderate volume.

## Limitations
Most studies last 6-12 weeks, use young men, and measure muscle thickness in one spot. Set counting rules differ (for example whether bench press counts for triceps). Individual response varies a lot.
