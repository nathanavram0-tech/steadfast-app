## Summary
Light, moderate and heavy loads build similar muscle when sets are taken close to failure. Heavy loads build more maximal strength. So the rep range is mostly a practical choice: pick what lets you train hard, safely and consistently.

## Key findings
- Schoenfeld 2017: low-load (about 60% of 1RM or less) and high-load training produced similar hypertrophy when sets went to failure; high loads won for 1RM strength.
- Lopez 2021: across low, moderate and high loads, hypertrophy was similar; strength favored heavier loads.

## Practical application
- Use about 5-30 reps per set for growth. Most sets in the 6-15 range are efficient: heavy enough to progress, not so many reps that fatigue and discomfort end the set early.
- Keep heavier work (about 4-8 reps) on compound lifts if you also want strength.
- Very high-rep sets (20-30) are fine for joints-friendly isolation work but are uncomfortable and easy to stop short of failure.

## Limitations
"Close to failure" matters: light sets stopped far from failure grow less. Most studies are in untrained or recreationally trained people over short periods.
