## Summary
The upper (clavicular) chest is best targeted with incline pressing. The one longitudinal trial comparing flat, incline and both found that only the incline group grew the upper chest clearly more. It is early evidence from untrained men, so treat it as a strong lean, not a law.

## Key findings
- Chaves 2020: 47 untrained men, 8 weeks, once a week. Incline-only pressing increased upper-chest thickness more than flat-only or a mix; other chest regions did not differ.
- EMG studies generally show more upper-chest activity on incline angles, but EMG does not reliably predict growth.

## Practical application
- Put an incline press (barbell, dumbbell, Smith or machine, roughly 30-45 degrees) first on at least one chest day, for 3-4 hard sets.
- Add low-to-high cable flyes or incline flyes for stretch under load.
- Keep total chest volume and progressive overload in place; angle choice only shifts where the growth goes.
- Check progress with photos from the same angle and light every 4-6 weeks.

## Limitations
Single small longitudinal trial in untrained men training once a week; results in trained lifters may be smaller. Upper-chest shape is also influenced by genetics and body fat.

## Conflicting evidence
Acute swelling data suggested flat bench may hit the upper chest similarly. Reviewers consider the longitudinal data more informative but still thin.
