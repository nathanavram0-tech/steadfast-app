## Summary
Muscle grows when its fibers are exposed to enough mechanical tension, often enough, with enough food and recovery. Schoenfeld's 2010 review proposed three drivers: mechanical tension, metabolic stress (the "burn" and pump) and muscle damage. Since then, most researchers treat mechanical tension on working fibers as the main driver; stress and damage are side effects that may contribute a little at most.

## Key findings
- Tension on the muscle fibers, especially when sets get hard enough that most motor units are recruited, signals the muscle to add protein.
- Soreness and damage are not required for growth. Chasing soreness can cut into how much quality work you can do the following days.
- The pump is a sign of blood flow and metabolites, not proof a set was productive.

## Practical application
- Judge a session by hard sets close to failure with good technique and a full range of motion, not by soreness or pump.
- Progress the load or reps over weeks so the tension stays high as you get stronger.
- Recovery (sleep, protein, a calorie surplus when bulking) is where the growth is actually built.

## Limitations
This is a narrative review from 2010. Its three-mechanism model is a framework, not a proven formula, and the weight given to each mechanism has shifted with newer studies.
