## Summary
The side (lateral) deltoid is what makes shoulders look wider from the front, so it drives the V-taper. In an 8-week within-person trial, 24 trained lifters did 5 sets of lateral raises to failure twice a week, one arm with cables and the other with dumbbells. Lateral delt thickness grew about 3-5% and the two tools produced essentially the same growth (Bayesian analysis favored "no difference"). Pick the version you can do with good form and progress over time.

## Practical application
- Treat side delts as their own muscle: presses mostly train the front delts, so add direct lateral raises.
- About 10-20 hard sets of lateral raises per week, spread over 2-3 sessions, fits the general volume research (see [[phys-weekly-volume]]).
- Cables or dumbbells both work. Cables keep tension near the bottom; dumbbells are simpler. Machines are fine too.
- Take sets close to failure with controlled reps (see [[phys-proximity-to-failure]]); add reps first, then load.
- Track shoulder width with the tape (shoulders measurement) and front photos every 2-4 weeks, since growth here is slow.
- Lower body fat makes shoulder width look more pronounced; leanness and delt size work together.

## Limitations
One 8-week study in 24 people measured with ultrasound; small and short. It compared tools, not the best weekly volume for delts specifically. The volume range is borrowed from general hypertrophy research, not delt-specific trials.
