## Summary
IGF-1 LR3 is a modified (long-acting) form of insulin-like growth factor 1. It is not an FDA-approved drug and is sold as a "research chemical". It is banned in sport: the 2026 WADA Prohibited List names IGF-1 and its analogues, including LR3-IGF-1, under S2.3 growth factors. Claims that it builds muscle in healthy people come from anecdotes, not controlled human trials.

## Key facts
- Regulators flagged many compounded peptides over safety concerns (immune reactions, impurities, hard-to-characterize ingredients, too little human safety data). In 2023 the FDA placed a group of peptides in its "Category 2" (significant safety risks) list for compounding; in April 2026 it moved several peptides out of Category 2 pending an advisory committee review, which does not mean they are approved.
- Products sold online are not quality-controlled; contents and sterility are unknown.
- IGF-1 activity affects blood sugar; low blood sugar is a known risk of IGF-1 drugs.

## Practical application
- The app never gives doses, schedules, combinations or ways to use these products.
- If he uses or considers them: do it only under a qualified doctor's supervision, with bloodwork (including glucose) and an honest discussion of risks; stop and seek care for symptoms like shakiness, sweating, confusion or chest pain.
- The proven levers for muscle (hard progressive training, enough protein and calories, sleep, creatine) carry far more evidence.

## Limitations
Regulatory status changes; re-check by the review date. This is safety and status information, not medical advice.
